# Problem Statement

## Context
Scotland's A&E access standard is that patients should be seen and then admitted, transferred or discharged within 4 hours. Public Health Scotland (PHS) publishes monthly figures for every Emergency Department.
Performance has fallen sharply since 2019: across Type 1 Emergency Departments, the share of patients seen within 4 hours dropped from about 90% in 2019 to about 62% in August 2026, and the share waiting over 12 hours rose from under 1% to over 5%.

## Problem
Operations managers can see their own board's numbers, but there is no simple, comparable view of which boards and sites are furthest behind, how far each has fallen from its 2019 baseline, and whether the problem looks like too much demand or poor patient flow. Without that, improvement effort is spread thinly instead of aimed where it matters most.

## Business questions
1. Which health boards and sites have the lowest share of patients seen within 4 hours?
2. How far has each board fallen from its 2019 baseline, and is it recovering?
3. Where are the longest waits (over 8 and 12 hours) concentrated?
4. Does higher attendance volume explain poor performance, or do similar-sized sites perform very differently?
5. Are there seasonal patterns that should shape winter planning?
6. How reliable is the data behind the rankings (unnamed sites, small sites)?

## Scope
- **In:** Type 1 Emergency Departments (full EDs), monthly PHS open data, July 2007 to August 2026, with focus on 2019 onwards.
- **Out:** Minor injury units and other Type 3 services (no 8/12-hour data), patient-level data, staffing and bed data, clinical causes, forecasting.

## Success measure
An operations manager can identify the five lowest-performing sites, and how each compares with 2019, in under two minutes using the dashboard.
